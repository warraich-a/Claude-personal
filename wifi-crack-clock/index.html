How an attacker would guess it

Attackers don't try every combination. They start with leaked passwords and dictionary words, then add capitals, years and symbols in the places people put them. This page splits your password into the pieces such a guesser would try and multiplies their odds.

    What this doesn't tell you

    My word list is small

    It holds a few hundred common passwords and words. Real attackers use lists with billions of entries. A name or word I don't recognise is scored as random letters, which makes it look stronger than it is. The page flags those letters below the breakdown.

    Personal guesses aren't modelled

    A neighbour who knows your street, your kids' names or your router's model will try those first. Any of them in your password is worth much less than this page shows.

    WPS skips the password

    If WPS PIN pairing is on, older routers can be broken through the 8-digit PIN in hours, whatever your password is. Turn WPS off in the router settings.

    WPA3 changes the maths

    WPA3-SAE doesn't leak anything that can be cracked offline, so the times here don't apply. Many routers run WPA2/WPA3 mixed mode, though, and older devices still connect over WPA2.

    Estimates follow the approach of Dropbox's zxcvbn: find the cheapest way to split the password into known patterns, then count the guesses. They are rough to within a factor of ten or so. Treat the colour as the answer, not the digits.
